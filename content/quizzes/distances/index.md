    - id: 2
      text: "51 Pegasi b was the first exoplanet confirmed orbiting a Sun-like star — a 'Hot Jupiter' that completes one orbit every 4.2 days. How close does it orbit its host star?"

        - text: "0.052 AU"
          image: "51-pegasi-b.jpg"
          correct: true
          explanation: "Correct! 51 Pegasi b orbits at just 0.052 AU — one-fifth of Mercury's distance from the Sun. Its 1995 discovery by Mayor and Queloz earned the 2019 Nobel Prize in Physics and forced a complete rethink of how planetary systems form."
        - text: "0.39 AU"

        - text: "5.2 AU"
          image: "jupiter.png"
          correct: false
          explanation: "5.2 AU is Jupiter's distance from our Sun. At this 'normal' gas giant distance, the planet would be cold — nothing like the scorching 1,000°C atmosphere of a Hot Jupiter."

    - id: 3
      text: "Metis is an inner moon of Jupiter that orbits inside the planet's Roche limit — the zone where tidal forces are stronger than a loosely-bound body's own self-gravity. How close does Metis orbit Jupiter's center?"
      image: "metis-moon.jpg"
      answers:
        - text: "71,500 km"
          image: "jupiter-cloudtops.jpg"
          correct: false
          explanation: "71,500 km is Jupiter's equatorial radius — that's the cloud tops of the planet itself. No moon could exist here; anything this close would be inside Jupiter."
        - text: "128,000 km"
          image: "metis-moon.jpg"
          correct: true
          explanation: "Correct! Metis orbits at 128,000 km — well inside Jupiter's Roche limit of ~175,000 km. It holds together only because it's a solid, coherent body. Debris shed from Metis contributes to Jupiter's faint main ring."
        - text: "175,000 km"
          image: "jupiter-rings.jpg"
          correct: false
          explanation: "175,000 km is Jupiter's Roche limit itself — the boundary beyond which moons can hold together as loose aggregates. The main ring lies within this zone, which is why it can't coalesce into a moon."
        - text: "422,000 km"
          image: "io.jpg"
          correct: false
          explanation: "422,000 km is the orbit of Io — Jupiter's volcanically active innermost Galilean moon. Io is well beyond the Roche limit; its volcanism is driven by tidal *heating* from orbital resonance with Europa and Ganymede, not tidal disruption."

        - text: "2,300 light-years"

        - text: "3,300 light-years"
