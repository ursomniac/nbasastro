## Central picture
A cluster that looks like a Christmas tree only once you know where to look, sitting in front of
(and inside) the cloud that made it. The tree is upside down in most telescopes and charts
[verify orientation conventions: apex = Cone Nebula, south]. The cluster is young enough that
star formation is still going on around it.

## Stories to dig for
1. **Two things, one NGC number.** NGC 2264 covers both the cluster and the nebula (Cone, Fox Fur)
   [verify how the NGC entry is defined; W. Herschel discoveries 1784/1785 — which part, which
   date, H VIII.5 / H V.27?].
2. **S Monocerotis — the star on top of the tree (the trunk, really).** Massive O-type binary,
   the brightest star in the field [verify type, mag ~4.7, variability].
3. **The Cone Nebula.** A dark pillar at the tree's tip; Hubble ACS image (2002) [verify] —
   public domain? (NASA/ESA images: check credit terms).
4. **How young?** Cluster age a few Myr [verify]; pre-main-sequence stars still contracting;
   many variables (T Tauri). The Spitzer "Snowflake Cluster" (embedded young stars) [verify].
   Hook: you're looking at stars that haven't reached adulthood.
5. **Distance** ~2,300–2,600 ly (≈ 700–800 pc) [verify, Gaia-era value]. Same general
   region as the Rosette (NGC 2244)? [verify — Mon OB1 vs Mon OB2].
6. **Hubble's Variable Nebula (NGC 2261)** — a few degrees away, R Mon; first Palomar 200-inch
   image (1949). Good aside or Observing Challenge spin-off [verify separation, story].
7. **Why the eyepiece shows a tree but no nebula** (and the smart scope shows nebula): the
   Fox Fur and Cone are faint emission/dark structure; Hα matters. Ties to the NGC 253 piece's
   broadband vs Hα theme.
