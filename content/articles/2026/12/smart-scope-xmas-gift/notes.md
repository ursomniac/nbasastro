## Angle (Bob)
Not "how to use it." The question is: you unwrapped one (or someone in the house did) — now
what cool things can it do? And where do you find targets that aren't in the app's catalog?
Encouragement first: "you can be the astronomer" (PHILOSOPHY) — a smart scope can do real
observing, not just pretty pictures.

## Central picture (proposal)
The app's built-in list is the front door. The sky's changing contents — comets, asteroids,
variable stars, supernovae, occultations — are the back rooms, and the keys are a handful of
free websites professionals and amateurs already use.

## "Cool things" — candidate sections (pick ~5–6)
1. **The showpieces first** (one paragraph): winter targets the app does well — M42, M45,
   NGC 2264 (ACL publishes Dec 27, after this — no link), Rosette, M1. Get a win on night one.
2. **Comets.** What's up in Dec 2026–Jan 2027 [check aerith.net weekly list + COBS]. Resource:
   Seiichi Yoshida's aerith.net. How to get coordinates in → the scope (RA/Dec goto, or
   the app's own comet list where it exists [verify per app: Seestar, Unistellar, Dwarf, Vaonis]).
3. **Asteroids / dwarf planets.** Watch one move over an hour or a night. All four of the first
   asteroids are up in late December (positions in `first-asteroids` notes). Pluto challenge
   (link the earlier OC). Resources: JPL Horizons (ephemerides), MPC. `first-asteroids`
   publishes Dec 21, before this — link back to it.
4. **Moons of Jupiter / mutual events.** Jupiter is a late-evening object in late December
   (rises ~8:30 PM) and moves into the evening through January; 2026–27 is a mutual-event
   season — a time-lapse of one moon eclipsing another is a genuine smart-scope project.
   Resources: IMCCE PHEMU campaign, BAA tables [verify which are friendliest].
   (`jupiter-returns` publishes Jan 10 — no forward link.)
5. **Supernovae in other galaxies.** Bright-ish ones (mag 12–14) are within reach. Resource:
   Rochester "Latest Supernovae" page; TNS [verify current URLs]. Before/after image of a host
   galaxy = a memorable result.
6. **Variable stars.** Seestar's variable-star mode (Bob testing) [verify what it does];
   AAVSO VSX / charts. Algol minima as an easy first light curve. (Hold back detail — the
   Feb 2027 variable-stars article owns it; mention only.)
7. **Citizen science.** Unistellar network (occultations, exoplanet transits) [verify current
   programs]; IOTA asteroid occultations. Possibly more than gift-week readers want — one para.
8. **Narrowband vs broadband** (the NGC 253 "experiment" idea): shoot both, compare.
