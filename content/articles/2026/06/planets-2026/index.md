## Introduction

The dizzying orbital motions of the eight primary planets
presents a never-ending game of watching them traverse 
across the sky, watching them disappear behind the Sun and
then wondering "when will they come back?"

## Say Goodbye! to Venus and Jupiter

While Venus and Jupiter put on a great show in the May and June evening
sky, Jupiter disappears behind the Sun 

## Say Hello! to Saturn and Neptune

Saturn and Neptune have been running through a series of conjunctions
the last year, but Saturn is now drifting eastward away from faint
Neptune.

For the rest of this year, Saturn is mostly in Cetus and Pisces.
Why Cetus?  This is an artifact of the IAU's definition of 
constellation boundaries - parts of the sky now ceded to Cetus
come really close to the Ecliptic, and since each planet has it's own
orbital inclination they will drift above and below the Ecliptic
slightly in their orbits as seen from the Earth.   

## Mars Makes a Late Appearance - so does Uranus

Mars has an orbital period that's 687 days (1.88 "Earth" years).
So if you look at its orbit compared to the Earth, at any given point
it looks like Earth is either speeding away from Mars, or trying to catch 
up to it and pass it.   When the Earth passes Mars (or any of the outer
planets) that's an "opposition" and that's the point when the planet 
is the closest it gets to Earth.

But for Mars oppositions happen every ~780 days (2.16 years) - why that's longer than the 1.88 years is because both Earth and Mars are moving.  The next opposition is 19 Feb 2027.  Until then Mars is slowly rising earlier and earlier each night, but also moving across the sky.   For the next few months, it remains
an early-AM object - not rising until about 4AM.   It won't become an evening
object until late in the year.

Likewise, Uranus is also an morning object, although it'll be an evening
object in the late Fall.   

## Mercury - Elusive as Always

Mercury (and Venus) bounce between being an "evening" star and a "morning" star.You'll hear it referred to as an "elongation".   At some point it'll reach the farthest extent from the Sun - the "maximum elongation".   Not all elongations are the same though: some are more favorable than others.
