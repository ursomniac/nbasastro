## 🌐 Our Website Has Launched!

We're really excited to announce our new website:  https://nbasastro.org  and hope it can be a regional "point of presence" for
astronomy and educational outreach!   The site offers:

* Articles spanning the cosmos from asteroids in the Solar System, to supernovae in distant galaxies
* Ongoing Series on particular topics
* Tools to help you plan your night time observing

Our "Solar System Dashboard" it a kitchen sink of information on:

* The locations of the Sun, Moon, and Planets, 
* Renderings showing the phases of the inner planets, and features on Mars 
* Timings to see Jupiter's Great Red Spot 
* The orientation of the satellite systems of Jupiter and Saturn, and 
* Keeping tabs on upcoming (hopefully) bright comets.

And there's even more!  From the site you can get:

* The current view of the constellations.
* Up-to-date observing/weather conditions.
* Upcoming passages of the International Space Station
* Aurora forecasting

Plus the NBAS calendar, almanac, and regional events (if you know of any we should know about - let us know!)

This globular is overshadowed by the competition, namely the Great Hercules Cluster (M 13),
but it's almost as bright: under very dark skies, like M 13, it is visible dimly to the 
naked eye.  While in binoculars and smaller telescopes, it only shows as a hazy patch, larger
telescopes (&geq; 6") will resolve individual stars: the brightest are 
about mag 10.5.  It also might be one of the younger globular clusters: only 
10.6 Gyr instead of the usual ~12.
