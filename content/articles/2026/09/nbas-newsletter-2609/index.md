## 🎂 Celebrating our 3rd Anniversary!

{{< nbas-image src="IMG_2608.webp" width="400" align="left" 
caption="NBAS observing in dark Vermont skies" >}}
Three years ago this month, NBAS started as a handful of Northern Berkshire astronomy
enthusiasts looking for an excuse to get together under dark skies. Since then we've put
out a newsletter every single month without missing an issue -- this is #36 -- alongside
36 constellation highlights, 18 Closer Look deep-dives, and everything from smart-scope
challenges to astronomy quizzes along the way. We've also taken the club on the road,
with public observing nights at Williamstown and partnerships with Arunah Hill
and Notchview that put telescopes in front of anyone curious enough to show up, plus
gathering for member observing nights at several dark-sky locations in the area.

None of that happens without the club's founding goals staying front and center:
promoting and teaching astronomy, welcoming hobbyists at every level, and making the
resources -- telescopes, cameras, know-how -- available to everyone who wants to look
up. Three years in, that's still the whole point. Here's to the next three.

## 🎤 Public Observing - Williamstown Historical Society - Sep. 12

{{< nbas-image src="wmtown.webp" width="400" align="right" 
caption="Come join us at our upcoming public event!" >}}
The Little Red Schoolhouse (historically the South Center School) is a beautifully preserved 1865 one-room schoolhouse. Located at 32 New Ashford Road (Route 7) in South Williamstown, MA (next to the Store at Five Corners), it currently serves as the home of the Williamstown Historical Museum.

We'll be there with a lecture by NBAS member Dr. Bob Donahue talking about "30 Years of Exoplanets" and observing through telescopes to follow.   (Saturn and Neptune will definitely be on the menu!  Maybe even Pluto in a smart scope!)

## 📸 This Month's Image
{{< nbas-image src="ic_10-93S-PIXRCAABEGHS-272m-square.webp"
caption="IC 10, Bob Donahue, NBAS, Two Seestar S50s, 272 min" >}}

Continuing the theme from last month using a pair of Seestar S50s to "gang up"
on an object, here's IC 10 - "the Starburst Galaxy" in Cassiopeia.

While the nickname evokes something bright and dynamic looking, the dim, hazy
galaxy is anything but.  But looks deceive - and particular here:  IC 10 is
actually one of the galaxies in the Local Group, and its true brightness is
masqueraded by the unfortunate coincidence that it's located very close to the
Galactic Plane.  So, it's shrouded by local gas and dust block much of the light
(in visible wavelengths) and also causing it to appear quite red.

For this challenge, the goal was "get as many images as you can in a single night",
and the Seestar S50s (Elusippe and Ellen) did their best - 1,633 images went into this
stack - that's just over 4&frac12; hours!
