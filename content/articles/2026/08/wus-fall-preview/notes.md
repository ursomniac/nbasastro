# WUS Plan: Fall 2026 Preview — "Best Easy-Access Objects" Buffet

Working list only — not the article draft. Series: `whats-up-in-the-sky`. **Publish date: August 20, 2026.** Window covered: fall evening sky (Sept–Nov), ~40°N.

## Angle (revised)

Not a smorgasbord of events — a buffet of the best/easiest accessible fall objects across naked-eye, binoculars, and small telescopes. Spirit is closer to "scanning the sky and noticing things" than the June Milky Way Binocular Tour's guided-path format. No deep stories/astrophysics — highlights only, geared to amateur/beginner observing experience. Saturn and the outer planets remain excluded (dedicated articles elsewhere); heavy planet content also isn't needed here since Spring's WUS already covered Venus/Jupiter/Mercury territory.

M31 and M33 are back IN for this piece — they were being deliberately avoided in the Sept 10 AWV "Fall Galaxies" article (which wants to cover galaxies *other than* M31/M33, possibly pivoting toward imager-focused content or Compact Groups instead). No conflict: that piece is deep/imager-oriented, this one is beginner/casual-observer buffet. Different audience, different treatment, same objects can appear in both without feeling redundant.

### Considered and cut

- **NGC 7027 (Cygnus, planetary nebula)** — cut per your "3 PNs is overkill" note. Kept NGC 6826 (Blinking Planetary — unique interactive averted-vision effect) and NGC 7662 (Blue Snowball — the one you specifically confirmed) since they're the most differentiated experiences; NGC 7027 was the most redundant of the three (small blue-green dot at high power, same basic experience as Blue Snowball). Facts stay on file if you'd rather swap it back in: mag 8.5, ~16″×12″, ~3,000 ly, near-stellar at low power, not in Messier/Caldwell/Herschel 400 — genuinely overlooked if that angle matters more than variety.
- **Iris Nebula, NGC 7023 (Cepheus)** — is Caldwell 4. Verified: this is mostly a photography target. The illuminating star (mag 7.4) is easy in any scope, but the nebula itself is a genuine stretch visually — faint wisps in 6″+ under dark skies, not a satisfying "wow" view for a beginner buffet. Recommend leaving out.
- **Helix Nebula, NGC 7293 (Aquarius)** — is Caldwell 63. Your instinct was right: despite a bright integrated magnitude (7.3, actually the brightest planetary nebula in the sky by that measure), its surface brightness is very low because it's so large (~20′). Genuinely hard in a scope — visible in 4″ as a faint oval, ring structure needs dark sky + filter. Aquarius is also only moderate-low altitude in fall evenings. Recommend leaving out.
- **NGC 6905, Blue Flash (Delphinus)** — confirmed NOT in Messier or Caldwell, so it does fit the "overlooked" ask, but at mag 10.9 it's the faintest candidate considered and really needs 6–8″+ to be satisfying. Left out of the main table as a bit too dim for a beginner-buffet pick, but worth keeping in your back pocket as a "bonus challenge object" if the article wants one.
