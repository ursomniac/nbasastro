## Throughline — fact pass result: HOLDS, and gets better

The irregular look is what gas turning into stars looks like in a shallow gravitational well. Take the gas away and the chaos goes with it.

What the fact pass changed:
- **Dropped for good:** "never had enough mass to form a disk." WLM's gas rotates, and it is modeled as an inclined (74°) rotating system with an exponential stellar disk. IC 1613's gas rotates slowly (about 25 km/s at most) but rotation still beats random motion by roughly 2:1. **[OK]**
- **New and strong:** IC 1613's hydrogen is full of holes and shells, 200–300 pc arcs expanding at 10–20 km/s, plus giant holes several hundred pc across and a ~1 kpc supershell. The patchiness is literally star formation blowing holes in the gas. (The largest supershell is too big for the observed star formation to explain; open question.) **[OK]**
- **New and strong:** even WLM, the most isolated dwarf in our set (~1 Mpc from both the Milky Way and M31; nearest neighbor the Cetus dwarf, ~250 kpc), is losing gas to ram pressure from the intergalactic medium (2026 paper). There may be no truly "undisturbed" dwarf. **[OK, single recent paper; word it as a recent result]**
- **Wrinkle:** NGC 1569 is not isolated. Its distance was revised to 3.36 Mpc in 2008, which made it an IC 342 group member and an even more extreme starburst. Whether a neighbor triggered the burst is NOT established here. **[OK for distance/group; trigger unverified]**

### Proposed order: a gas gradient, from simmer to boil to empty
1. **WLM** — quiet, isolated, rotating, patchy anyway. Starting point.
2. **IC 1613** — quiet, patchy because star formation punches holes in its gas.
3. **IC 10** — the Local Group's only starburst, behind our own dust.
4. **NGC 1569** — the extreme starburst; a group member after all.
5. **NGC 185** — near M31, a little gas left (2.5×10⁵ M☉ of gas, some dust), star formation only near the center in the last few hundred Myr. Smooth.
6. **NGC 147** — near M31, no gas or dust detected, nothing new in at least the last ~1–3 Gyr. Smooth. The end state.

That replaces "cameo" for NGC 147/185 with a place in the sequence; they are still the contrast case.
