# Article 7: Building the Ladder (working title)
**Slug:** building-the-ladder | **Series:** HP+AF | **Position:** 7 of 13
(NEW in v3. The lower rungs of the distance ladder — the tools anchored directly on parallax.

**VERIFICATION STATUS:** from memory unless marked [verified, source].

## Core Hook
Parallax runs out fast — in 1900 it reached a few dozen parsecs at best [UNVERIFIED]. To go
farther you need a different kind of tool: something you can see far away, plus a connection
back to something parallax has already measured. That's what a rung is. This chapter builds
the lower rungs, shows how each one rests on the one below, and — because every rung has
broken at least once — shows the break and the repair, so the reader leaves with the ladder
as it actually stands.

## Central picture
Every method here answers one question: **how bright is it, really?** Once you know a star's
true brightness, the −5 formula (parsecs-vs-light-years) gives its distance.

## Structure (draft beats)

### 1. Herschel's mistake (the hook)
- Star gauges, 1785: he treated EVERY star as a standard candle (same true brightness).
  Short; the map itself is galactic-scale's, and the H-R diagram article has the story —
  link back. Echo of his "distance of Sirius" unit (parsecs-vs-light-years).
- The ladder is the search for stars that really ARE standard.

### 2. What a rung is
- Something visible far away + a connection back to something already anchored. Calibration
  means finding SOME of them near enough for parallax.

### 3. Statistics instead of single angles: Kapteyn
- Secular / statistical parallax: the Sun's motion as a baseline that grows every year;
  average distances for groups [UNVERIFIED details]. (Kapteyn's MODEL of the Galaxy is
  galactic-scale's.)

### 4. Clusters: the Hyades (room for this — Bob: few people know this story)
- Moving-cluster method: shared motion + perspective → distance [UNVERIFIED details].
- Main-sequence fitting: line up a cluster's H-R diagram with the Hyades' → distance. The
  Hyades becomes the zero point: "one of the foundations on which the extragalactic distance
  scale ultimately rests" (Perryman et al., astro-ph/9707253) [verified, old #7 notes].
- Link back to the H-R diagram article (CMDs).
- **Break / repair:** the Pleiades — Hipparcos parallaxes ~10% short of main-sequence fitting
  (Pinsonneault et al. 1998; Narayanan & Gould; Robichon et al. 1999) [verified, old #7
  notes]; a spatially correlated Hipparcos systematic; the Hyades sat where errors cancelled.
  Gaia's verdict [UNVERIFIED — check]. Back-link: Hyades and Pleiades as landmarks
  (parsecs-vs-light-years).

### 5. One star at a time: spectroscopic parallax
- Spectrum → type and luminosity class → true brightness → distance. Calibration is coarse
  (luminosity class sets M) — honest about the limits (Bob). Bounded: the tool only; its use
  on star counts is galactic-scale's.

### 6. Leavitt's law
- Cepheids in the Small Magellanic Cloud, all at about the same distance → apparent brightness
  tracks true brightness → period–luminosity relation (1908 notes, 1912 paper) [UNVERIFIED].
- Keep lean: center on Cepheids, not the Harvard background. Point readers to Dava Sobel,
  *The Glass Universe* (2016), and George Johnson, *Miss Leavitt's Stars* (2005).
- "Who published vs who measured" (Pickering) [UNVERIFIED — check before using].
- The catch: a slope with no zero point — no Cepheid near enough for parallax.

### 7. Hertzsprung sets the zero point; RR Lyrae
- 1913: statistical parallaxes of Galactic Cepheids → calibration [UNVERIFIED]. (SMC distance
  misprint story — check before using.)
- RR Lyrae: same idea for older stars; nearly the same true brightness [UNVERIFIED].
- Present Cepheids as understood 1913-1952: one relation, hard to calibrate. No modern
  correction slipped in before the callout.

### CALLOUT: Baade, 1952 — the rung that was two rungs
(The series' one big loop — house rule 4. Told here so the reader never handles the broken
rung.)
- Two kinds of Cepheid (classical and Type II), different period–luminosity relations; early
  calibrations mixed them [verified, old #7 notes: Caltech E&S 1953, Encyclopedia.com, NASA
  Cosmic Times, physicstoday.aip.org].
- Found with the 200-inch at Palomar; announced at the 1952 IAU meeting in Rome
  [verified, same]. How it showed up: stars that should have been visible in Andromeda at the
  old distance weren't [UNVERIFIED detail].
- Consequence in one line: distances measured with Cepheids beyond the Galaxy roughly doubled
  (check exactly what doubled before wording it). Baade/Shapley credit dispute: one sentence.
- Only the distance part: "two kinds of Cepheid, two calibrations." Not stellar populations.

### 8. The correction every rung needs: dust
- Trumpler 1930 [verified, galactic-anticenter notes: Lick Obs. Bull. 420]: two distance
  methods on the same open clusters (brightness vs angular size) disagreed, and the gap grew
  with distance → absorption. Ladder logic: two rungs checked against each other.
- Every brightness-based rung needs an extinction correction from then on.

### Ending
The lower ladder as it stands: parallax → clusters → Cepheids and RR Lyrae, with dust
corrected and the two kinds of Cepheid separated. Everything after this uses it.
