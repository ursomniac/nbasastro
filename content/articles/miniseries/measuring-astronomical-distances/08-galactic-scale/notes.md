## Core Hook
With the lower rungs in hand, we can finally say where we are in the Milky Way. But knowing
the Sun is 8 kpc from the center isn't the same as having a map. Parallax reaches only part of
the disk, and dust blots out most of the rest. So how do we map the far side of a galaxy we're
stuck inside? And what is the Galaxy like as a PLACE — from its nearly empty halo to its
crowded center? Be strategic: a few well-chosen comparisons, not everything against
everything (Bob).

### 4. The Galaxy as a neighborhood — how crowded is it?
Density AND average spacing (n^−1/3); nearest neighbor ≈ 0.55 × spacing. Keep stars/pc³ and
M☉/pc³ apart.
- Solar neighborhood: 0.080 systems/pc³, spacing 2.3 pc, median nearest neighbor 1.26 pc
  [verified, computed from Reylé et al. 2021/2023, CDS J/A+A/650/A201]. Back-link.
- Halo near the Sun: ~0.5% of the local thin-disk density; ρ ∝ r^−2.8; c/a = 0.64 [verified,
  Jurić et al. 2008]. ~12 pc spacing; ~70 pc at 50 kpc (my extrapolation — label it). Bob:
  "70 pc" works as a comparator once 10 pc is felt.
- Bulge: [NEED a real source].
- OPEN CLUSTERS (own section): densities computed from Cantat-Gaudin 2020 (Bob has table1).
  Weak source only so far: Pleiades ~3/pc³, M36 ~12, M11 ~83 [verify]. Gallery (Bob's images):
  Pleiades · M44 · M36 · M37 · M11 — order by computed density.
- GLOBULAR CLUSTERS (own section — "now when you go to globulars it changes like this"):
  densities from Harris 2010 (Bob's mwgc.dat). M15 core ~4×10^6 stars/pc³ (secondary source;
  verify). Planet-in-a-core thought experiment (Spurzem, astro-ph/9804089). Gallery: M71 or
  NGC 5466 · M3 · M13 · M92 · M15 — order by computed density.
- Galactic center: >10^7 M☉/pc³ within 0.1 pc of Sgr A* (Schödel & Eckart 2007); 2.8×10^6
  M☉/pc³ at the break radius (Schödel et al. 2007). Mass, not star count.
