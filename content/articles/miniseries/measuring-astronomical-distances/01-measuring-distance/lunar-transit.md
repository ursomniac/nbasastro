| Mission | Outbound Transit (Launch to Lunar Orbit Entry) | Return Transit (Lunar Orbit Exit to Splashdown) | Mission Notes & Trajectory Context |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apollo 10 | 75 hours, 55 minutes | 54 hours, 27 minutes | Full dress rehearsal for landing. Reached a record return speed of 24,791 mph relative to Earth during re-entry. |
| Apollo 11 | 75 hours, 49 minutes | 63 hours, 55 minutes | First lunar landing. Total time from launch to touchdown on the lunar surface was 102 hours, 45 minutes. |
| Apollo 12 | 78 hours, 25 minutes | 48 hours, 9 minutes | Precision landing in the Ocean of Storms. Shorter return transit due to a highly efficient Trans-Earth Injection (TEI) burn. |
| Apollo 13 | N/A (Did not enter orbit) | ~86 hours (Post-accident return)| Service Module oxygen tank exploded 56 hours in. Used a lunar free-return trajectory to swing around the Moon and head home. |
| Apollo 14 | 81 hours, 56 minutes | 67 hours, 26 minutes | Alan Shepard became the oldest person to walk on the Moon. Outbound trajectory adjusted slightly to clear Earth launch delays. |
| Apollo 15 | 78 hours, 31 minutes | 71 hours, 23 minutes | First "J-mission" featuring a heavier payload, including the Lunar Roving Vehicle (LRV). Extended orbital stay windows. |
| Apollo 16 | 74 hours, 28 minutes | 70 hours, 30 minutes | Target landing in the Descartes Highlands. Trajectory modifications accounted for troubleshooting a backup gimbal motor. |
| Apollo 17 | 86 hours, 14 minutes | 67 hours, 49 minutes | Final Apollo lunar mission. Longest outbound transit due to an intentional 2-hour, 40-minute launch delay on Earth. |
| Artemis II | ~115 hours (About 4.8 days to Moon's sphere of influence) | ~96 hours (About 4 days from lunar flyby to Earth) | First crewed lunar flyby of the Artemis program. Spent 24 hours in a high Earth orbit checking life support before the Trans-Lunar Injection (TLI) burn. Set a new absolute human distance record by traveling 252,756 miles from Earth. |
