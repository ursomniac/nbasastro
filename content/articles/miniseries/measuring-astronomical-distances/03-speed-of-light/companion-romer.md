(EXERCISE / BOOK SEMI-CHAPTER: "You Can Measure the Speed of Light Yourself"
 - Next Jupiter opposition: ~11 Feb 2027 (computed min distance; VERIFY official date).
   Article publishes 8 Nov 2026, so the timing works.
 - Cumulative delay after opposition (computed, vs. a constant-period clock):
     +30 d (13 Mar):  ~1.2 min   ~17 orbits
     +60 d (12 Apr):  ~4.2 min   ~34 orbits
     +90 d (12 May):  ~8.0 min   ~51 orbits   <- the sweet spot
     +120 d (11 Jun): ~11.8 min  ~68 orbits
   The curve is FLAT near opposition and steepens after -- key reader insight: the
   night of opposition tells you almost nothing; the payoff comes 2-4 months later.
   Mirror image before opposition (eclipses run EARLY): -90 d (13 Nov 2026) ~7.9 min.
 - Worked check: 8.03 min / 0.966 AU = 8.3 min per AU. Method works on modern data.
 - Visibility: before opposition you see Io DISAPPEAR into the shadow; after, you see
   it REAPPEAR (Wikipedia; Rømer's figure). So the 2027 project = timing emersions,
   Feb-June, evening sky. VERIFY with a moon-events predictor.
 - THE TRAP (from the 2026 replication paper): published eclipse predictions (apps,
   almanacs, Horizons) ALREADY include light-time. Compare your observations to them
   and you'll find nothing. The reader must build their own constant-period clock from
   near-opposition timings (or use the mean period and one anchor eclipse). The paper
   notes its own high-precision result was "a timing-consistency check against known
   physics, not an independent first-principles determination" -- same trap.
 - Precision reality: 8 min of signal vs. timing errors of ~1 min is fine. Io takes
   a few minutes to fade (it crosses the shadow edge at ~17 km/s and is ~3,600 km
   across -- VERIFY, and whether that means ~3.5 min fade); time a consistent point.
 - Precedent: Falchi, Furgoni, Gattillo, Francesio, "De mora luminis: Roemer's
   discovery 350 years later," arXiv:2607.28512 (July 2026): 80-115 mm refractors,
   emersions over ~70 orbits; simple circular-orbit model gave ~259,000-268,000 km/s
   (~10%). NOTE: read via a summary page only; arXiv was unreachable this session --
   read the paper itself before citing. Also reports Rømer checked his results with
   Great Red Spot transits (1677) -- interesting, verify.)
