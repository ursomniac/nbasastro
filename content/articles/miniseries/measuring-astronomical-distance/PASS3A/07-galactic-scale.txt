## Core Hook
Parallax — even with Gaia — reaches only a fraction of our own galaxy. So
how do we know where the spiral arms are, where the center is, and what's on
the *other side* of the galaxy, when we're stuck inside the disk looking
through it? Answer: a patchwork of tracers and methods, each getting around
one of two walls (geometry and dust) — and an honest admission that our map
of our own galaxy is still incomplete.

## Structure (draft beats)

### 1. Why parallax runs out — two walls, not one
- **Wall 1: geometry.** Parallax angle shrinks with distance; even Gaia's
  precision covers only part of the disk (and that fraction is increasing
  with each data release). Get actual Gaia reach figures — UNVERIFIED.
- **Wall 2: dust.** Along the galactic plane, visible light is heavily
  extinguished. It's not just that the angle gets small — we can't see the
  stars at all in visible light. Everything after this is about getting
  around one wall or the other.

### 2. Why the kpc — the parsec's zero-conversion trick, one scale up
- d[pc] = 1/parallax["], therefore d[kpc] = 1/parallax[mas].
  1 mas = 1 kpc; 0.1 mas = 10 kpc. (CERTAIN — follows from the definition.)
- Direct payoff of parsecs-vs-light-years: the unit stays convenient
  because it's tied to what's actually measured.
- Galaxy numbers stay human-sized in kpc (Sun-center distance, disk size,
  arm spacing) — get figures, UNVERIFIED.

### 3. Globular clusters and the Sun thrown off-center
- Shapley (~1918): globular cluster distances show they're centered on a
  point far away toward Sagittarius, not on us — moved the Sun far from the
  galactic center. UNVERIFIED (date, method details).
- Twist: his galaxy came out too big, partly from ignoring dust.
  UNVERIFIED.
- Trumpler (~1930): discovered interstellar extinction — open cluster
  distances from angular size disagreed with distances from brightness.
  UNVERIFIED.
- Herschel's star-gauging map (already in the published H-R diagram
  article) assumed no dust and put us near the center; Kapteyn's model had
  the same flaw. Ready-made Herschel -> Kapteyn -> Shapley -> Trumpler arc.
  UNVERIFIED (Kapteyn details).
- Color-magnitude diagrams / main-sequence fitting for cluster distances —
  see boundary question with ladder-cracks.

### 4. Spiral arms — mapping a forest from inside it
- Arms are traced with things that only exist where stars are forming:
  OB stars, HII regions, young clusters, molecular clouds. UNVERIFIED as a
  complete list.
- **Kinematic distances** (the workhorse): measure a gas cloud's velocity
  (21-cm HI, CO), assume a rotation curve, convert velocity to distance.
  UNVERIFIED (details).
- **The near/far ambiguity:** inside the Sun's orbit, one velocity matches
  two distances; the measurement alone can't tell which. This is the
  direct answer to "what about the other side?" — a whole class of
  distances is ambiguous by construction. UNVERIFIED.
- Infrared standard candles (red clump giants, Cepheids, RR Lyrae) for
  seeing through dust; infrared surveys revealing the central bar.
  UNVERIFIED.

### 5. The far side — parallax comes back, at radio wavelengths
- Masers in star-forming regions measured with VLBI radio interferometry:
  true trigonometric parallax, at a wavelength that passes through dust.
  BeSSeL survey and VERA (Japan). UNVERIFIED.
- At least one far-side star-forming region measured by parallax (recalled
  as ~20 kpc; recalled as Sanna et al. 2017) — UNVERIFIED, must check.
- Beat: the oldest method (parallax) solves the hardest mapping problem once
  it changes wavelength.

### 6. The distance to the galactic center — measured by an orbit
- Star S2 orbiting Sgr A* gives a geometric distance to the center (~8 kpc;
  recalled as ~8.2 kpc from GRAVITY) — UNVERIFIED.
- Kepler again: the same orbital reasoning that handed us the AU (see
  measuring-the-au), now at 8 kpc. Fits the "early framework" throughline.

### 7. Ending — honest about what we don't know
- Our own galaxy's spiral structure is still debated; recalled that even
  the number of major arms (two vs. four) isn't settled — UNVERIFIED.
- We have clearer pictures of some distant galaxies' arms than of our own,
  because we're inside ours.
- Hand off to ladder-cracks: leaving the galaxy (Andromeda, the Mly rung).
