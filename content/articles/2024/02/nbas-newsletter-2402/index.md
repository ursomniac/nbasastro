## Moving Towards Spring

{{< nbas-image src="becky_pontier.jpg" fullwidth="true"  
caption="Becky Pontier, NBAS" >}}

The days are getting longer, but despite the cold and snow, as we
move out of the winter Milky Way, there are (as always) lots of
things to observe.

Looking to the East we move away from the winter objects in
Orion, Taurus, and Canis Major (although they’re all still
observable), and into Gemini and Cancer, but also
Camelopardalis and Lynx - both of which are full of deep-sky
objects generally overlooked because of their dim parent
constellations. As you’re musing about what to look at, don’t
forget to check out this area of the sky.

{{< clear >}}

## Looking Above

Turning attention away from Orion, there are several exciting
objects to see with small telescopes.

Here’s a finding chart for all four of these and other interesting objects.

{{< nbas-image src="nbas_2402_finder-88.png" fullwidth="true"  >}}

{{< clear >}}
### Clown in the Cold
{{< nbas-image src="c_39-92U-PIXRCAGRAX-GHS-cropped.jpg" width="350" align="right" >}}
C 39 (NGC 2392) is a small planetary nebula with a lot of
structure under high magnification. It is a good “challenge” object.

{{< clear >}}
### "Twinkling Comet"
{{< nbas-image src="ngc_2420-92U-PIXRCAGRAXGHS-square.jpg" width="350" align="right" >}}
No, it’s not a comet, but cluster NGC 2420 has a tendency to
pop in and out of view in a small telescope. Try it!

{{< clear >}}
### Wanderer?
{{< nbas-image src="c_25-other.jpg" width="350" align="right" >}}
C 25 (NGC 2419) is a distant globular cluster that takes 3
billion years to orbit the Milky Way.

{{< clear >}}
### Dusty Hand
{{< nbas-image src="ngc_2146-93U-PIXRCAABEGHS-square.jpg" width="350" align="right" >}}
NGC 2146 is in Camelopardalis has an odd shape, and is bright
enough to see with a moderate aperture telescope.

{{< clear >}}
## This Month's Image

{{< nbas-image src="b_33-92U-PIXRCAABEGHS-full.jpg" caption="Bob Donahue, NBAS" >}}

The Horsehead Nebula is something of a “right of
passage” for amateurs because it’s challenging to detect visually
but well worth it.

Here, the dust cloud of the dark nebula (3 1/2 light years across)
is situated in front of an illuminated emission nebula (IC 434). 
It’s 1,753 light years away.
