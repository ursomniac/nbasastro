## Galaxy Season

Springtime means galaxies! With the Milky Way mostly below the
horizon, the evening skies are pointing out of the plane of the
Galaxy, and towards the Virgo supercluster. There are several that
are bright enough to see with small telescopes:

### Virgo Galaxies

{{< nbas-image src="constellation-virgo-star-chart.jpeg" >}}

There are far too many galaxies in Virgo to discuss. Scanning the
sky East of Denebola (β Leonis), even small telescopes will uncover
many, like those in the Markarian Chain (which includes M 84 and M 86).

Further S, above Corvus and W of Spica, you can find the “Sombrero”
Galaxy, M 104 with its prominent dust band.

{{< clear >}}

### Near the Big Dipper

{{< nbas-image src="M101_Finder_Chart.png" >}}

Likewise, there are several bright galaxies in the skies 
near the handle of the Big Dipper!

M 106, M 51, M 109 are all popular.

### Elusive Pinwheel

{{< nbas-image src="Messier-83.jpg" width="350" align="right" >}}

Very low in the sky, is the beautiful Southern Pinwheel galaxy,
Messier 83, sitting on the Hydra/Centaurus border.

{{< clear >}}
