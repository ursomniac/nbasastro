## Introduction

Buying your first telescope is exciting, but it can also be
overwhelming. Walk into any astronomy retailer (or browse online
for five minutes) and you'll encounter a wall of unfamiliar terms — refractors, 
reflectors, Dobsonians, alt-az mounts, focal ratios,
Barlow lenses — and prices ranging from $50 to $5,000. It's a lot.

{{< nbas-image src="guy_with_telescopes2.png" fullwidth="true" >}}
 
This guide is here to help. We'll walk you through what you actually
need to know before spending a dollar, in plain language, in a
logical order. By the end, you'll understand what the different
types of telescopes are, what they're good at, what accessories
matter, and how to make a smart first purchase — new or used.
 
A few things to keep in mind as you read:
 
- **There is no perfect telescope.** Every design involves tradeoffs. 
We'll help you understand them so you can decide what matters most to you.
- **Aperture is king.** The single most important spec on any telescope is 
the diameter of its main optical element. More on that in the next section.
- **The best telescope is the one you'll actually use.** A big, complicated scope 
that sits in a closet is worth less than a modest one that comes out every clear night.
